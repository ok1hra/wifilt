How the data are compared and what the result means

Active segments. Segments are aligned to UTC and evaluated separately per band. With the filter on, only segments where both antennas have at least one spot are kept. A zero count may mean a receiver that was off, but also just weak reception. The filter can therefore suppress a real sensitivity difference; try longer segments or turn it off. Segments with no spots on either side are not counted.

Log sessions. If the logs contain a gap longer than one hour, the time range is split into sessions that can be selected with one click. Manual start/end entry still works.

Pairing. Same time, band, callsign (without brackets), transmit power and a compatible locator where both sides have one. Frequency must be within the set tolerance; the closest frequencies take precedence and each spot is used once. Receiver clock offset is not corrected. Several transmissions of the same callsign in one cycle are told apart by frequency. “Only A/B” means unpaired, which does not always mean unheard.

Duplicates and missing locators. Exact duplicates of time, callsign, frequency, locator and power within one file are merged, keeping the highest SNR. Records without a locator count towards reception and pairing, not towards distances. Other diagnostic columns of ALL_WSPR.TXT are not distance.

Interpreting SNR. The main SNR difference and median are computed from shared pairs only. Averages over all decoded spots describe a different set of signals and are not a direct measurement of antenna gain. Results are affected by receivers, settings and local noise. Δ is a difference in dB, not a percentage. Times in the data and filters are UTC.

Distances. Great-circle distance on a sphere of radius 6,371 km from the centre of the Maidenhead locator. A four-character locator is a rough position estimate. Both antennas are assumed to be at the same receiving site. The histogram uses equal 2,000 km bins.
